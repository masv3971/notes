1Part 1. Why a trusted list

ProblemA signature proves that a key signed. It does not prove that you should believe the owner of the key.
TakeawayTrust is not in the signature. It is in the list that the signature is checked against.

Any key can sign. The question is whose key it is. A trusted list answers that with one signed document.

"How do you decide today that a certificate is from the right party?"

Part 1 is about the list. Part 2 is about a different answer to the same question.

Handout section 3Slide 2 · 5 min S

2Trusted lists: three kinds

Trusted List

One per Member State

ETSI TS 119 612. Lists trust service providers and their services. In the wallet world it carries the anchors of QEAA providers.

LOTL

The index

List Of Trusted Lists, run by the Commission. Same format. Points to every national list and to the certificates that sign them.

LoTE

For wallet roles

List of Trusted Entities, ETSI TS 119 602. Commission-published anchors for PID Providers, PuB-EAA Providers, Wallet Providers and access CAs.

TakeawayThe LOTL finds the lists. The Trusted Lists and LoTEs hold the anchors.

Do not mix up the LOTL, which is a list of Trusted Lists, with the LoTE, which is a list of trusted entities for the wallet.

Trusted Lists come from eIDAS 1. LoTEs are new for the wallet. Same idea, new roles.

The LoTE format details come from ETSI TS 119 602, which I could not read. The roles come from the ARF.

Handout section 5Slide 4 · 3 min

4Inside a Trusted List

TrustServiceStatusList SchemeInformation TSLType, TSLSequenceNumber ListIssueDateTime, NextUpdate PointersToOtherTSL LOTL only TrustServiceProviderList TrustServiceProvider TSPInformation TSPServices TSPService ServiceInformation type, status, certificate ServiceHistory Signature XAdES
Service type

One URI per kind of service, for example CA/QC, TSA/QTST or EAA/Q.

Status

Current: granted or withdrawn. Earlier statuses, such as undersupervision, stay in the history.

Signature

XML signature over the whole list. The LOTL uses exclusive canonicalization and RSA with SHA-512.

The format is old and verbose. What matters is: provider, service, type, status, certificate.

The Swedish list even has an EAA/Q service type. The wallet era is already in the format.

Element names come from the live files and general TS 119 612 knowledge. Check ServiceHistory against the spec.

Handout section 6Slide 5 · 3 min S

5Using the lists

1. AnchorGet the LOTL signer certificates from a source you trust, such as the Official Journal.
2. LOTLDownload it. Check its signature. Read the pointers.
3. ListsDownload each list. Check it against the signer certificate in its pointer.
4. EntriesChoose services by type and status. Keep their certificates as anchors.
5. RefreshDownload again before NextUpdate. Drop anchors whose status changed.
TakeawayOne anchor from outside. Everything else is checked from there.

The verifier needs one thing from outside the system: the signer certificates for the LOTL.

Everything after that is a signature check. A verifier can then work from its cache.

"What happens to your verifier if the list is not refreshed for a year?"

Step 1 is the usual practice. The exact publication place is in the Commission decision. Verify before you promise it.

Handout section 8Slide 7 · 4 min S

7Part 2. OpenID Federation

Same problemA verifier meets a party it has never met. Whom does it believe?
TakeawayNo central list. Each party signs about itself. Each authority signs about those below it.

The names come from the specification's own example. Umea University's login server, vouched for by Umea, then SWAMID, then eduGAIN.

The hint is only a pointer to someone who may vouch. The proof is in the signatures.

Federation was built for exactly our world: universities, federations, many members.

Handout section 9Slide 8 · 4 min

8Two kinds of statement

Entity Configuration: iss = sub
iss, sub same entity iat, exp short lifetime jwks its federation keys metadata what it is and does authority_hints who vouches trust_marks badges it holds
Subordinate Statement: iss ≠ sub
iss the authority sub the entity below jwks keys of the sub metadata_policy rules for the sub constraints limits for the sub source_endpoint where it came from
TakeawayBoth are signed JWTs, typed entity-statement+jwt, signed with a key from the issuer's jwks.

The left one is a self-portrait. The right one is a reference letter from someone above.

Keys are in the statements. The specification avoids web PKI on purpose.

Handout section 10Slide 9 · 5 min S

9The trust chain

Build

Follow authority_hints. Fetch a Subordinate Statement at each step.

What the verifier holds

Only the trust anchor: its entity id and its keys, from outside the system.

Checked at every link

iss of one is sub of the next. The signature fits a key in the next statement. iat is past, exp is future.

The chain from the bottom up: op.umu.se, umu.se, swamid.se, edugain.

The verifier does not need the middle parties in advance. It finds them by following hints.

The chain's lifetime is the shortest exp of any statement in it.

"Where is the one thing you must get from outside?" Answer: the anchor's keys.

Handout section 11Slide 10 · 3 min S

10Rules that flow downward

"metadata_policy": { "openid_relying_party": { "grant_types": { "subset_of": ["authorization_code"] }, "contacts": { "add": ["helpdesk@org.example.org"] } } }
Policy operators

value, add, default, one_of, subset_of, superset_of, essential.

Hierarchy

No one below can loosen a policy. A conflict makes the whole chain invalid.

Constraints

max_path_length, naming_constraints, allowed_entity_types.

This is what a list does not have. An authority can say what its members may claim, not only who is a member.

The example is from the specification, shortened.

Handout section 12Slide 11 · 2 min

11Trust marks

Example from the specswamid.se issues a Sirtfi trust mark to umu.se/op.
What

A signed badge: this entity meets a named requirement. It has a type, an issuer and a subject.

Who may issue

The trust anchor lists accepted issuers per type in trust_mark_issuers. An empty list means anyone.

Still valid?

Check exp, or ask the issuer's status endpoint: active, expired, revoked or invalid.

Trust marks add a second layer: not only who you are, but what you comply with.

13Part 3. Compare: shape

Aspect
Trusted list
OpenID Federation
Shape
One flat signed document, plus an index of such documents
A tree of signed statements, one per entity and per relationship
Format
Trusted Lists and the LOTL: XML with XAdES. LoTEs have a JSON data model
JWTs with JWS signatures, keys as JWKs
Anchor
Signer certificates of the LOTL, from outside
Entity id and keys of the trust anchor, from outside

Same question, two shapes. One big document you download. Many small statements you collect.

14Compare: who decides

Aspect
Trusted list
OpenID Federation
Publisher
Member States, and the Commission for the LOTL and LoTEs
The federation operator, intermediates, and each entity about itself
Getting in
Notification and supervision give a status in the list
A Subordinate Statement from your immediate superior
Legal weight
eIDAS: qualified status comes from the list
None by itself. Each federation defines what membership means

The ARF requires a verifier to check a qualified attestation against a Trusted List. That is law, not choice.

Federation says how parties get to know each other. What membership means is for the federation to decide.

15Compare: finding and caching

Aspect
Trusted list
OpenID Federation
Start
The LOTL, then every national list
The entity identifier of the party you check
Direction
Top down: download all, keep the anchors
Bottom up: build a chain for one entity on demand
Offline

Federation also has a list endpoint for top-down discovery, and a resolver endpoint that does the chain work for you.

Using a resolver means you trust the resolver to do the checks.

Handout section 15Slide 16 · 3 min S

16Compare: freshness and failure

Aspect
Trusted list
OpenID Federation
Update
Sequence number and NextUpdate on the list
exp on every statement. Fetch again when it nears
Removal
Status changes to withdrawn. Verifiers must re-download
No revocation process is defined. Statements expire or are dropped
Keys
New certificate in the service entry
jwks in the statements. Optional historical keys endpoint
Risk
Stale list kept in use past NextUpdate
Unreachable endpoints. Many fake authority_hints as an attack

The federation specification says it defines no revocation. It relies on frequent checks and short lifetimes.

For lists the risk is quiet: nothing breaks, the data just gets old.

Handout section 16Slide 19 · 4 min S

19Rule of thumb

A list fits when

Legal status matters. Parties are few and change slowly. Verifiers must run from cache.

Federation fits when

Many entities join and leave. You need rules for metadata. Registration is delegated, as in universities.

Both in one verifier

Choose the framework per role. Qualified providers by list. Sector services by federation.

TakeawayA list says who is allowed. A federation also says who vouches and under which rules.

This is a rule of thumb, not a law. The honest answer is: it depends on who carries the responsibility.
